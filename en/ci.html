Addressing Objections

6 Common Questions About Critical Illness Insurance

Q1. I already have provincial healthcare and group benefits. Do I still need individual coverage?

Provincial healthcare and employer group benefits primarily cover doctors and in-hospital care. Costly targeted drugs, home nursing, out-of-province medical travel, and ongoing wage replacement are often not fully covered, and group benefits usually end if you leave your job. Calculate how much your existing coverage handles and where the gaps remain first, then see how much personal CI is needed to bridge them.

Q2. Do I receive the payout immediately upon diagnosis?

It is not automatically paid out the moment a diagnosis occurs. Existing policies require a confirmed diagnosis of a covered condition and surviving the contract's survival period. The length and definition of the survival period depend on the specific illness and contract and cannot be generalized. Whether there is a waiting period or exclusion period also depends on the contract wording. An advisor will point out this specific clause in your contract so you understand how claims actually work.

Q3. What if the condition diagnosed is not on the covered list?

The Sun Life plan featured here covers 26 full-payout severe conditions and 8 partial-payout early-stage conditions (partial payouts pay 15% of coverage up to $50,000 without reducing your base sum), plus 5 childhood-specific conditions. These numbers reflect this specific plan design, not every CI policy on the market. Exactly which conditions qualify for full or partial payout and how they are defined are governed strictly by the policy contract.

Q4. Premiums are significant—if I stay healthy, did I lose all that money?

The Return of Premium (ROP) feature works like this: if you remain healthy and make no full claim, you can choose to surrender the policy at a specified milestone (such as after 15 years or at age 65) to receive a 100% refund of all paid premiums. Partial claims typically do not affect your ultimate right to a full premium refund. However, this refund is only received if you choose to surrender at that specific time, which also ends your coverage—it is not an interest-bearing savings account. Specific milestone years, ages, and conditions are subject to your contract.

Q5. What if I have pre-existing conditions or family history—will I just be declined?

Do not self-disqualify beforehand. Underwriting evaluates each applicant's individual medical background. An advisor can submit preliminary inquiries on your behalf. Never assume you cannot be insured, and never omit medical history during application. The final outcome is determined by official underwriting review.

Q6. Should I buy Critical Illness or Term Life first?

This retains the original educational question from this page: Term life provides capital for your loved ones after you pass, while critical illness provides capital for yourself to use while surviving severe illness. They serve two entirely different purposes. The most comprehensive plan usually combines both. This educational principle is not a personal recommendation; the exact combination should be calculated with an advisor based on your personal protection gaps and budget.
