Addressing Objections

6 Common Questions About Participating Whole Life Insurance

Q1. Are policy dividends guaranteed every year?

No. Dividends are based on carrier performance and are not contractually guaranteed. What is permanently guaranteed is your basic death benefit, scheduled premiums, and guaranteed cash values. Dividends represent potential, not a promise. While top Canadian carriers have paid dividends annually for over a century, past performance does not guarantee future results. Specific terms are governed by your policy contract and annual dividend declarations.

Q2. Is this an investment, and how does its return compare to stocks?

It is an insurance product, not a pure market investment, and should not be compared directly to equity market returns. Policy cash values grow in a tax-preferred, tax-deferred environment, but growth is not guaranteed. The 6.0% figure in the calculator is an illustrative scenario assumption, not a guaranteed return. Your decision should be based on your overall planning objectives, not on chasing short-term returns in any given year.

Q3. Do I have to pay premiums for life? What if I can no longer afford them?

Not necessarily. The Quick Pay (Accelerator) plan allows you to fund premiums in just 8 years for lifelong coverage, and other plans offer varied payment schedules detailed in your illustration. It is vital to understand the contractual consequences of missed payments before purchasing—you should never assume you can stop paying anytime without affecting your coverage.

Q4. Is the money locked in? Can I access it in an emergency?

You can access accumulated cash value through policy loans or withdrawals to supplement retirement, fund education, or handle emergencies. However, policy loans accrue interest and reduce your death benefit and cash value, while withdrawals may trigger tax consequences. Accessing capital is not free; speak with an advisor and your accountant regarding the exact impact.

Q5. Does everyone actually save the hundreds of thousands shown in the tax calculator?

Not everyone. The numbers in the calculator reflect a specific set of illustrative assumptions, not your personal after-tax result. Whether and how much you save depends on your personal or corporate marginal tax rate, corporate vs. personal ownership, adjusted cost base (ACB), and other factors. Final tax determinations rest with your licensed accountant—advisors cannot provide tax guarantees.

Q6. How do I choose between Protector, Accumulator, and Accelerator?

These three plans represent different focuses within the same carrier series, not a cross-carrier comparison. Sun Par Protector II focuses on securing higher lifetime death benefits per premium dollar for estate legacy; Accumulator II generates higher cash values in early policy years; Accelerator is designed to be fully paid up in 8 years. Your choice depends on your specific goals and cash flow capacity—an advisor will compare personalized illustrations with you rather than choosing based solely on names.

How Can You Use Policy Dividends? (Dividend Options)

Four common dividend choices set up with your advisor based on your financial goals—not a single default recommendation

🚀

Paid-Up Additions (PUA)

Reinvest dividends to automatically buy more death benefits and cash value—compounding gains over time (best for maximizing long-term protection and value).

📉

Premium Reduction

Direct dividends to automatically offset your annual out-of-pocket premium costs.

💵

Cash Payouts

Receive direct annual cash distributions for passive cash flow (withdrawals may trigger tax consequences).

🏦

Accumulation on Deposit

Leave dividends with the carrier to gain stable interest rates similar to standard savings accounts.
