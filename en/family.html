The 3 Most Common Gaps at This Stage

When risks materialize, families don't face policy terminology—they face non-negotiable monthly expenses.

01

Income Disruption

If one breadwinner falls ill or passes away, the surviving partner must manage the family while continuing to cover bills and payments. Life cannot pause.

02

Mortgage & Fixed Overhead

Mortgage, property taxes, auto loans, insurance, and utilities are charged monthly. They do not pause because a family experiences hardship.

03

Recovery & Time-Off Costs

When diagnosed with a severe illness, you may be unable to work for months. Rehab, extra caregiving, and reducing workloads all require liquid cash.

Methodology

How to Think About Your Coverage Gap

Common Estimation Approach

Mortgage Balance + Monthly Expenses × Months to Cover + Child Education Reserve - Existing Group Benefits & Savings = Net Protection Gap to Consider

📝 Hypothetical Example (Formula demonstration only, not coverage advice):

Suppose a household has a remaining mortgage of $600,000, monthly essential living expenses of $5,000, and wishes to secure a 3-year (36 months) transition cushion of $180,000; subtracting an existing employer group life coverage of $200,000:

$600,000 + $180,000 - $200,000 = ~$580,000 Baseline Responsibility Gap

*Note: This simplified example does not factor in existing liquid savings, a spouse's independent income, post-secondary education funds, or existing personal policies. Exact coverage depends on your personal financial needs analysis (FNA) and underwriting.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q1 I have group benefits through work. Do I still need individual insurance?

Group plans are an excellent foundation, but limits are typically tied to your salary (e.g. 1–2x annual pay) and may end or alter if you switch employers. Critical illness and disability coverage may also be limited. Most people use group insurance as a baseline and top up what's missing, rather than treating it as an either-or decision.

Q2 What is the core difference between Life Insurance and Critical Illness?

Simply put: Life insurance provides funds to your loved ones if you pass away. Critical illness insurance provides funds to you while you are alive and recovering from a major illness. Disability income replaces a portion of earnings month-by-month if you cannot work. Because they address different financial problems, they are often reviewed together.

Q3 How much coverage is enough?

There is no one-size-fits-all number. Key variables include your remaining mortgage, monthly fixed overhead, target years of coverage, kids' ages, and existing assets. Always calculate the gap first before evaluating products.

Q4 What if I have some health conditions? Can we still discuss options?

Health conditions impact underwriting, but they certainly do not preclude coverage. Many conditions qualify for standard rates or specific exclusions, while others require reviewing medical history. Don't rule yourself out beforehand—talk it through first, and base decisions on the underwriter's actual response.
