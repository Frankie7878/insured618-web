Clarity First

6 Common Questions About Health Insurance

Q1. I have provincial healthcare. Do I still need private health insurance?

Provincial healthcare primarily covers doctor appointments and hospital visits. The four key categories shown on this page — prescription drugs, dental care, vision exams, and ambulance services — are typically not covered by provincial plans. Private health insurance bridges these specific gaps rather than duplicating basic medical coverage.

Q2. Does private insurance reimburse 100% of drug and dental expenses?

No. Coverage depends on the tier and annual maximums. Basic plans require adding dental riders, while comprehensive and premier tiers offer higher limits and lower co-pays. Exact percentages and co-pays are defined by the specific plan tier you select, rather than covering every bill in full.

Q3. What if I have pre-existing conditions? Will I be declined or excluded?

It depends on the path you choose. Personal Health plans require a medical questionnaire, with pricing based on health status, and pre-existing conditions may have limited coverage or higher premiums. Replacement Health plans offer guaranteed acceptance with no pre-existing condition exclusions, provided you apply within 90 days of leaving your group benefits, though they come as fixed packages. Do not self-disqualify, but do not assume guaranteed acceptance is available outside that 90-day window.

Q4. I just left my job or retired and lost my group benefits. What should I do?

Do not delay. Replacement Health plans require application within 90 days of your group coverage termination. Once this window closes, guaranteed acceptance is no longer available. Confirm your exact termination date, calculate the 90 days, and an advisor can verify eligibility.

Q5. How much does it cost? What if I pay premiums but rarely use it?

Pricing depends on your age, province of residence, chosen plan tier, and (for Personal plans) health history. You can get an instant quote online. When choosing a tier, evaluate your actual annual spending on prescriptions, dental, vision, and therapy: lower tiers have modest monthly premiums but lower coverage caps, while higher tiers have higher premiums but significantly lower out-of-pocket costs.

Q6. Do I have to pay upfront and file claims? What about travel outside my province?

Not always. Your health card doubles as a Pay-Direct Card, allowing participating in-network pharmacies, dentists, and massage therapists to bill the insurer directly. However, not all providers belong to the network, so verify before treatment. Regarding travel, ambulances and accidental dental emergencies are typically not covered out-of-province by public healthcare. Replacement plans at the Choice and Premier levels include 15-day out-of-province/country emergency travel coverage, which addresses a separate need from routine provincial healthcare gaps.
