Addressing Concerns

6 Most Common Concerns About Term Life Insurance

Q1. Worried it's too expensive and out of budget?

Initial term life premiums are generally much lower than permanent insurance because it only covers a specific duration without a cash savings component. Within your chosen term, premiums remain fixed according to contract. Once the term ends and renews, rates adjust based on contractual terms.

Q2. Already have group insurance through work—do you still need personal coverage?

Group life insurance often covers only about 1 to 2 times your annual salary and terminates if you switch jobs. It usually cannot cover the full lifespan of your mortgage and children's education. Calculate how much your group plan covers and what the remaining gap is first, then see how much personal term coverage is needed—it doesn't have to be either/or.

Q3. How long should the term be? Worried about picking the wrong duration?

Look at when your financial gap concludes: how many years remain on the mortgage, and how many years until the kids become independent. Standard market examples are 10, 20, or 30 years; Evolve offers terms from 5 to 40 years, while Essential offers 5 to 20 years. Duration is selected based on your timeline of need, not solely by initial price.

Q4. What if you want to switch to permanent insurance later and worry about being locked in?

Clarify this upfront, as the plans differ significantly: Sun Life Evolve can be converted to permanent life insurance before age 75 with no medical underwriting, and is renewable up to age 85. Sun Life Essential cannot be converted to permanent coverage and cannot be renewed. Not all term policies are convertible, so future flexibility should be factored into your decision today.

Q5. Have pre-existing health conditions or diabetes—worried about immediate rejection?

There is a dedicated program for diabetes offering accessible coverage, reasonable premiums, diabetes management resources, and the Achievers reward program that helps you maintain preferential rates by meeting health targets. Higher approval likelihood does not guarantee acceptance—final decisions rest with carrier underwriting—so don't self-disqualify prematurely; let an advisor explore your options first.

Q6. If nothing happens when the term ends, was the money wasted?

Term life is pure protection: if no claim occurs during the period, there is no payout—which is precisely why its premiums remain economical. You are purchasing financial security and high leverage for that vulnerable window, not a savings account. If your goal is receiving money back or lifetime coverage, that represents a different product objective, and an advisor will evaluate both separately rather than just comparing price tags.
